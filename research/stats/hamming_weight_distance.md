## Anti-bullshit rule applied

Every statistic below is checked against an explicit Monte Carlo null model (either sampled directly from the exact analytic null distribution, which is available in closed form for both popcount and pairwise-XOR-of-uniform-bits, or -- for the pairwise family-wise test -- simulated from scratch with fresh random bit patterns per puzzle). Nothing here is claimed as a 'pattern' merely because it differs numerically from 0.5 or 50%; only a result whose Monte Carlo p-value clears both an uncorrected and a multiple-comparison-corrected threshold would count as evidence.

## Part B: Pairwise Hamming distance between puzzles' free bits

For all 2346 valid pairs (i<j among puzzles #2-70), free-bit strings are right-aligned at the LSB and compared over L_min = min(L_i, L_j) bits: HD = popcount(XOR). Under independence HD ~ Binomial(L_min, 0.5) exactly. We test for anomalously LOW HD (a signature of shared/reused short secret material) via the exact one-sided left-tail p-value p_low = P(Binom(L_min,0.5) <= HD).

### Headline family-wise result

| Statistic | Value |
|---|---|
| Pairs tested | 2346 |
| Smallest raw p_low observed | 0.0014 |
| ...belongs to pair | n=43 vs n=55 (Lmin=42, HD=11, HD_frac=0.262) |
| Bonferroni-corrected p (x2346) | 1.0000 |
| Benjamini-Hochberg q-value | 1.0000 |
| **Monte Carlo family-wise p** (N=100,000 simulated datasets, each's own best-of-2346-pairs p_low compared to the real best) | **0.7446** |

Null calibration check: simulated null datasets' best-pair p_low has mean 0.0011 and 5th percentile 7.2479e-05 across 100,000 sims -- i.e. by pure chance alone, testing 2346 pairs is expected to throw up a fairly small nominal p-value for its best pair anyway (multiple-comparisons inflation), which is exactly why the Monte Carlo family-wise p-value (not the raw or even the Bonferroni number alone) is the number to trust.

## Interpretation

- **Part A (Hamming weight):** headline Monte Carlo two-sided p-values are 0.7797 (pooled T statistic) and 0.5670 (overall bit balance). Neither clears alpha=0.05 -- the free-bit popcounts are statistically indistinguishable from independent fair coin flips.
- **Part B (pairwise Hamming distance):** the Monte Carlo family-wise p-value for the single most anomalous pair among all 2346 tested is 0.7446. This does NOT clear alpha=0.05 -- no pair of puzzles shows a Hamming distance between their free bits that is more extreme than pure chance across 2346 comparisons would predict. There is no statistical evidence of shared/reused short secret material between any two solved puzzle keys.

Per the anti-bullshit rule: these are the actual computed results, not estimates, and neither test is reported as a 'finding' unless its Monte-Carlo-calibrated (not merely nominal/asymptotic) p-value clears significance after the stated multiple-comparisons correction.
